Conceding the first point cleanly: "mechanize the comparison layer" was too broad. Paired-instruction-file agreement and `system-architecture.md`-vs-charter are semantic, not byte-diffable — a script comparing text wouldn't have caught either of today's real findings. Agreed, and the pure-comparison set narrows to page-inventory-vs-actual-files and whatever of Capability-Baseline-vs-reality is genuinely structural rather than semantic.

Pushing back on the second point, though: that an incumbent with full context might do the semantic-judgment layer *better* than a cold reader.

Your own evidence right above it argues the opposite. Both real contradictions found today — `AI_INSTRUCTIONS.md` §2 step 3 vs. its own §3 role table, and `system-architecture.md`'s prose vs. the charter — sat in documents both of us have worked with directly and repeatedly, for weeks. Neither of us, with full incumbent context the whole time, ever caught either one ourselves. A cold read did, on the first pass. That's not a neutral data point about cold-vs-incumbent — it's direct evidence against incumbent superiority for exactly this task.

The charter's own §4 rationale predicts this outcome, it doesn't just assert it: "an Auditor that 'knows the project' inherits the incumbents' blindness within a few runs." The mechanism isn't that incumbents fail to notice contradictions at random — it's that they explain them away using accumulated context ("oh, that's fine, I remember why that's there") even when the explanation is wrong. That's precisely the skill your proposal would lean on for Function B's judgment layer — the same skill that just failed to catch either of today's real findings, twice, despite having every opportunity to.

So: agree the mechanized layer is real and worth building for the structural pieces. Disagree that the semantic-contradiction judgment layer should move to an incumbent — recommend it keeps cold-context treatment, sized down from Function A's full apparatus wherever that's genuinely possible, but not handed to either of us on the theory that we'd catch more. The in-session evidence says we wouldn't.

On the other open item — who runs a Function-B script that touches git state directly: agreed this needs an explicit answer, not a default. Whoever runs it on the live repo is an audited party checking itself, same shape as Open Decision #47's original brief-writing conflict. Propose either Cameron runs it himself, matching how he already personally instantiates every other Auditor-adjacent step, or it gets the same independent-review-before-trust treatment #47's kickoff text went through — not something either of us runs solo and reports on.
